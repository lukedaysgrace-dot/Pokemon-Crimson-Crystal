# Move, ability and high-priority state audit — November 12, 2026

Status: complete. Both ROMs build; all final validation listed below passed.

This audit found and fixed nine game defects: eight battle mechanics defects
and one defensive storage API error. It also corrected an obsolete portrait
coverage test. The starting source revision was
`7ea05c0435f23a278d441b53d53702df4eec41f7`.

The review covered move execution and replacement, priority and guard state,
status changes, ability suppression and bypass, on-hit reactions, party-slot
history, held-item eligibility, indexed item storage, capture, PC transactions,
save migration and backup handling, Day-Care caps and EXP RELAY. Existing
documented custom mechanics and the save layout were preserved.

## Confirmed game defects, fixed

All battle findings below are P2 correctness defects. The storage finding is
P3: its invalid-input contract was wrong, but no normal UI data-loss workflow
was reproduced.

| Finding | Reproduction and cause | Correction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Baneful Bunker avoided the consecutive guard penalty | Repeated Bunker with a high RNG roll kept protecting both sides. Move selection preserved the counter only for Protect and Endure effects, resetting it before each Bunker. Switching from Protect, Endure or King's Shield to Bunker had the same problem. | Preserve the shared counter for `EFFECT_BANEFUL_BUNKER` in normal player selection, enemy selection and the Giga Hammer player reselection path. |
| Baton Pass downgraded the incoming mon's bad poison | Pass into an independently badly poisoned reserve. Entry restored that reserve's Toxic slot bit, but the later Baton Pass cleanup cleared the active Toxic flag. | Restore the replacement's own poison history after discarding the outgoing flag. Controls ensure the passer's bad poison is never transferred. |
| Disable accepted an exhausted move with PP Ups | A last-used move with raw PP `$40` has zero PP and one PP Up. Disable tested the whole byte and treated it as usable. Both directions reproduced the defect. | Mask the PP byte with `PP_MASK` before checking eligibility. |
| Non-Ghost Curse rejected available stat changes | At maximum Attack and Defense, Curse failed even when Speed could still fall. It also rejected available Contrary-reversed changes. | Check all three caps in the effective ability's actual change directions. Neutralizing Gas controls verify suppression. |
| Contrary Curse lowered Speed | Curse used the raw `LowerStat` helper for its own Speed change, bypassing Contrary. Its Attack and Defense changes used the ordinary ability-aware path. | Route the self-inflicted Speed drop through the shared stat command. Tests cover neutral stages, maximum Attack/Defense, maximum Speed and complete caps on both sides. |
| Poison Touch bypassed Covert Cloak | A contact hit poisoned a Cloak holder on either side. The ability checked Shield Dust but omitted the item exception. | Check the effective opponent item, retaining the expected behavior when Klutz disables the Cloak. |
| Delayed Yawn incorrectly retained Mold Breaker bypass | Mold Breaker could initially make a Leaf Guard holder drowsy in sun, then also bypassed its immunity when sleep resolved at end of turn. That second event is outside the move. | Use the effective, unignored ability for delayed sleep while retaining ordinary suppression. |
| Safeguard raised after Yawn canceled pending sleep | Land Yawn, then raise Safeguard before its countdown ends. A redundant Safeguard check at sleep resolution canceled the already-landed effect. | Keep the Safeguard check at drowsiness application; remove the late check. Both before/after controls are covered in both directions. |
| An unloaded PC update reported success | `UpdateStorageBoxMonFromTemp` with `wTempMonSlot = 0` returned Z from `and a`, despite its failure-return contract. | Return NZ without writes. New party/box variants also check that mon data, pointers and allocation state remain unchanged. |

The implementation changes are in `engine/battle/core.asm`,
`engine/battle/abilities_engine.asm`,
`engine/battle/move_effects/{baton_pass,disable,curse,new_move_cores}.asm` and
`engine/pc/storage.asm`. No ROM0 code, save offsets or saved identifiers changed.

## Regression coverage and baseline evidence

The three new battle files contain 45 native cases:

- `tools/battletest/tests/102-ability-audit-2026-11-12.yaml`: 16 cases.
- `tools/battletest/tests/103-move-audit-2026-11-12.yaml`: 20 cases.
- `tools/battletest/tests/104-ordering-audit-2026-11-12.yaml`: 9 cases.

`tools/test_pc_storage.py` adds eight assertions for unloaded updates.
`tools/test_battle_picture_tiles.py` now includes Entei's native picture frames.
`AGENTS.md` records the corrected invariants.

Before fixes, the guard file produced seven failures and two passing controls
(`.tmpbuild/bug-audit-2026-11-12/guard-baseline.log`). Move probes reproduced
eight failing scenarios, including both sides of bad poison, exhausted Disable
and Curse caps. The initial 12 ability probes had four failures and eight
passing controls; a later partial-build probe independently reproduced the
Safeguard/Yawn defect. The separate uncapped Contrary Speed defect was also
identified directly in the raw-helper call and is covered by neutral-stage
cases. The unloaded-update tests reproduced exactly two failures and 128
passes on each pre-fix ROM.

The preexisting ROMs were stale relative to the starting source, so baseline
results identify the ROM hash used, rather than claiming they reproduce the
entire starting revision. The source cause was verified for every change.
The final source was rebuilt with RGBDS 0.5.2, then a second build confirmed
both outputs were up to date.

## Additional findings and build outputs

- The old portrait test required dialogue for every retained portrait asset.
  Agatha and Will are intentionally inactive under the current League roster.
  The test now permits those retained assets explicitly, checks they still
  exist, and continues requiring every active portrait's dialogue coverage.
  All ten portrait checks pass.
- Normal builds regenerated `data/maps/portrait_texts.asm` and Entei's tracked
  animation tables/map. The portrait generator sorts Lorelei's rows and removes
  the redundant explicit no-portrait row for the Mt. Silver sign. Entei's graphics/map
  reproduce all 15,680 source pixel indexes exactly; restoring only the old
  tables would mismatch the rebuilt tile data. The generated outputs are kept.
- The earlier report's missing Hisuian Arcanine PNG warning is stale: both
  source images are already tracked at the starting revision.
- The previous Imposter/Neutralizing Gas hypothesis is resolved without a
  change: Imposter is switch-in-only and does not activate when Gas ends.
- Existing text issue, reported without dialogue changes as instructed:
  completely capped Curse prints “ABILITY won't rise anymore!” through
  `GetStatName` and `WontRiseAnymoreText`. Its mechanics now fail only when all
  three changes are capped.
- Retained legacy interaction, not classified as a confirmed defect: Ghost
  Curse is blocked by a target's Substitute, matching Gen II–IV. Modern games
  bypass it. The project mixes older and newer move rules and does not document
  a choice for this interaction, so this remains a design question. The primary
  [Gen IV move override](https://github.com/smogon/pokemon-showdown/blob/master/data/mods/gen4/moves.ts)
  makes the historical distinction explicit; local documentation also cautions
  against assuming modern Growth behavior (`docs/trainer-theme-audit-round5.md`).

The preexisting modification to `audio/ded/axew.ded` and the two untracked title
image `Zone.Identifier` files were left alone.

## References and limits

Expectations were compared against the primary
[Pokémon Showdown move implementation](https://github.com/smogon/pokemon-showdown/blob/master/data/moves.ts)
for Yawn, Safeguard and Curse, and its
[ability implementation](https://github.com/smogon/pokemon-showdown/blob/master/data/abilities.ts)
for Poison Touch's item exception and Imposter's activation rule. Local
documented custom rules take precedence.

The broad matrices provide execution coverage and many prepared mechanical
assertions; they do not prove every prerequisite of every possible interaction.
The separate all-move/every-ability Cartesian audit, a full campaign playthrough
and physical-hardware validation are outside this pass.
