Abilities on or off

Custom change
Gen 2
Pokémon have no abilities.
Crimson Crystal
Choose whether Pokémon abilities are On or Off when starting a save. With Off, no Pokémon on either side has an ability in battle or after it, the summary screen shows none, and Professor Elm does not hand out the ABILITY CAP.

Rules saved with your adventure

Custom change
Gen 2
No type/stat selection.
Crimson Crystal
Your independent typing, stat and ability choices persist in the save. Move changes, evolutions and battle mechanics apply with any selection.

Hard Mode forces Set battles

Custom change
Gen 2
Shift or Set can be selected.
Crimson Crystal
Hard Mode removes the free switch prompt after an opposing Pokémon faints. Normal retains the battle-style option.

Hard Mode limits Bag use in battle

Custom change
Gen 2
Battle items can be used from the Bag.
Crimson Crystal
Only Ball-pocket items can be used from the battle Bag in Hard Mode. Healing items remain usable outside battle; held items still work.

Hard Mode level caps

Custom change
Gen 2
No badge-based player level cap.
Crimson Crystal
Badge-based caps run through Johto, the League and Kanto. Johto caps are 10, 16, 20, 25, 30, 34, 38 and 45; the League cap is 57. Kanto progresses from 58 to 73.

Hard Mode experience bonus

Custom change
Gen 2
Trainer battles award 1.5× base experience.
Crimson Crystal
Hard Mode trainer battles award 2× base experience, with progression constrained by the current level cap. Normal keeps the 1.5× trainer bonus.

Hard Mode opponent training

Custom change
Gen 2
Trainer stats do not use this class-based training system.
Crimson Crystal
Trainer classes receive tailored stat experience in Hard Mode. Major opponents receive more training than ordinary route trainers; Normal uses zero trainer stat experience.

Battle calculations & turn order

Physical / special split

Modern rule
Gen 2
A damaging move’s category is determined by its type.
Crimson Crystal
Each move has its own physical or special category. Physical moves use Attack and Defense; special moves use Special Attack and Special Defense.

Critical-hit damage

Modern rule
Gen 2
Critical hits deal roughly double damage.
Crimson Crystal
The critical-hit damage multiplier is 1.5×.

Critical-hit rates

Modern rule
Gen 2
The base critical-hit rate is about 1/16.
Crimson Crystal
The base rate is about 1/24; +1 stage is 1/8, +2 stages is 1/2, and +3 or more guarantees a critical hit.

Focus Energy critical stages

Modern rule
Gen 2
Focus Energy adds one critical stage.
Crimson Crystal
Focus Energy adds two critical stages. High-critical-hit moves add one stage; abilities and held items can add more.

Damage variation retains Gen 2 rounding

Special exception
Gen 2
Ordinary damage rolls span approximately 85% to 100%.
Crimson Crystal
The game retains this range and the legacy byte-based rounding, rather than uniform modern 16-value damage rolls.

Enemy stat-lowering moves

Modern rule
Gen 2
Enemy stat drops can suffer an additional random failure.
Crimson Crystal
Opponent-inflicted stat drops do not have the extra Gen 2 computer failure roll. Accuracy, immunities and protection still apply.

End-of-turn effect order

Modern rule
Gen 2
Residual effects use the older turn flow.
Crimson Crystal
Effects resolve in phases, with effective Speed ordering within each phase. Trick Room reverses that ordering; weather, healing and status damage resolve consistently.

Modern priority moves

Modern rule
Gen 2
Priority uses the original move set.
Crimson Crystal
New moves and abilities have their own priority. Extreme Speed has +2 priority, Prankster adds +1 to status moves, and Triage adds +3 to supported healing moves.

Fairy type

Modern rule
Gen 2
Gen 2 has no Fairy type.
Crimson Crystal
Fairy is a fully supported type. It is immune to Dragon, weak to Poison and Steel, and super effective against Fighting, Dragon and Dark.

Steel no longer resists Ghost or Dark

Modern rule
Gen 2
Steel resists Ghost and Dark.
Crimson Crystal
Ghost and Dark attacks deal neutral damage to Steel before any second type is considered.

Critical hits ignore unfavorable stat stages

Modern rule
Gen 2
Gen 2 checks Attack and Defense stage relationships together.
Crimson Crystal
Critical hits ignore the attacker’s negative offensive stages and the defender’s positive defensive stages independently. Favorable offensive stages and lowered defenses still help.

Badge stat boosts remain

Special exception
Gen 2
Johto badges increase particular player battle stats.
Crimson Crystal
The game retains badge-linked stat boosts in ordinary single-player battles. Link battles and the Battle Tower skip these boosts.

Status conditions & type immunities

Freeze becomes frostbite

Custom change
Gen 2
Freeze prevents the Pokémon from acting.
Crimson Crystal
Frostbite allows normal actions, halves Special Attack and deals 1/8 maximum HP damage at the end of each turn.

Frostbite persists until cured

Custom change
Gen 2
Frozen Pokémon can thaw naturally.
Crimson Crystal
Frostbite does not thaw at random. Using the Pokémon’s own Fire moves does not cure it.

Frostbite protection and healing

Custom change
Gen 2
Ice typing and anti-freeze effects protect against freeze.
Crimson Crystal
Those protections now apply to frostbite. Ice Heal, Ice Berry and full-status cures remove it; Magma Armor prevents or cures it. Harsh sunlight blocks move-inflicted frostbite.

Burn damage

Modern rule
Gen 2
Burn deals 1/8 maximum HP each turn.
Crimson Crystal
Burn deals 1/16 maximum HP each turn and still halves Attack, subject to abilities such as Guts.

Paralysis Speed reduction

Modern rule
Gen 2
Paralysis reduces Speed to one quarter.
Crimson Crystal
Paralysis halves Speed. Its 25% chance to prevent an action remains.

Electric types resist paralysis

Modern rule
Gen 2
Electric typing does not provide blanket paralysis immunity.
Crimson Crystal
Electric-type Pokémon cannot be paralyzed, including by non-Electric moves or secondary effects.

Grass types block powder moves

Modern rule
Gen 2
Grass typing does not block powders.
Crimson Crystal
Grass types are immune to Poison Powder, Stun Spore, Sleep Powder, Spore and Cotton Spore. The immunity applies with either Grass type slot.

Powder immunity cannot be bypassed

Modern rule
Gen 2
There is no Grass powder immunity.
Crimson Crystal
No Guard and Mold Breaker do not bypass Grass typing’s powder immunity. The AI recognizes the immunity when choosing moves.

Powder Snow is still an attack

Special exception
Gen 2
Powder Snow is a damaging Ice move.
Crimson Crystal
Powder Snow remains a damaging Ice move and is not blocked by Grass powder immunity.

Effect Spore and Grass typing

Modern rule
Gen 2
Abilities do not exist.
Crimson Crystal
Grass types are also protected from Effect Spore’s contact-triggered status.

Ghost types escape trapping

Modern rule
Gen 2
Ghost typing does not exempt a Pokémon from trapping.
Crimson Crystal
Ghost types can leave trapping effects and trapping abilities.

Sleep duration

Modern rule
Gen 2
Ordinary inflicted sleep uses the older 2–7 turn roll.
Crimson Crystal
Ordinary sleep uses a 1–3 turn duration. Rest retains its own fixed sleep duration, and abilities can modify waking.

Confusion retains its stronger self-hit chance

Special exception
Gen 2
Confusion has a 50% self-hit chance.
Crimson Crystal
This game retains 50%, rather than the later main-game 1/3 chance.

Overworld poison can still cause fainting

Special exception
Gen 2
Poison damages the party while walking and can cause fainting.
Crimson Crystal
Walking poison damage and poison fainting remain in this game. Do not assume the later games’ 1-HP safety rule.

Dark types block opposing Prankster status moves

Modern rule
Gen 2
Abilities do not exist.
Crimson Crystal
Dark types are immune to opposing status moves boosted by Prankster. The user can still use Prankster moves targeting itself or its own side.

Bad poison persists through switching

Modern rule
Gen 2
Switching converts bad poison into regular poison.
Crimson Crystal
A badly poisoned Pokémon remains badly poisoned after switching. Its escalating damage counter resets when it re-enters battle.

Weather & field effects

Hail weather

Added feature
Gen 2
Hail does not exist.
Crimson Crystal
Hail is supported and deals 1/16 maximum HP damage to non-Ice Pokémon unless an ability protects them. Blizzard hits without its normal accuracy check in hail.

Hail also boosts Ice Defense

Special exception
Gen 2
No hail or snow Defense boost.
Crimson Crystal
Ice types receive a 1.5× Defense boost against physical moves in hail. This game combines damaging hail with the later snow-style defensive benefit.

Sandstorm boosts Rock Special Defense

Modern rule
Gen 2
Sandstorm does not boost Rock-type Special Defense.
Crimson Crystal
Rock types receive a 1.5× Special Defense boost against special moves during sandstorm.

Weather lasts five or eight turns

Modern rule
Gen 2
Weather moves use the original duration rules.
Crimson Crystal
Weather moves and weather abilities set five turns of weather, extended to eight by the matching weather rock.

Weather suppression

Modern rule
Gen 2
No ability-based weather suppression.
Crimson Crystal
Cloud Nine suppresses weather effects while its active holder is alive. The underlying weather remains and resumes when suppression ends.

Trick Room

Added feature
Gen 2
Trick Room does not exist.
Crimson Crystal
Trick Room reverses ordinary Speed order for five turns. Using it again ends it early; priority still matters.

Map-seeded weather has a longer duration

Special exception
Gen 2
No broad overworld-to-battle weather transfer.
Crimson Crystal
Weather inherited from the map starts with a 255-turn timer, effectively lasting the battle. A weather move or ability can replace it with its own normal duration.

Ability directory

Ability activation banners

Custom change
Gen 2
Pokémon have no abilities.
Crimson Crystal
Battle ability activations use a compact black panel with white text and clipped inner corners. Player banners slide in from the left and enemy banners from the right, joining the screen edge without a gap. The Pokémon’s possessive name and its ability are both left-aligned with matching padding. The panel fits the longer line and resizes when an ability is replaced, such as with Trace. The longest names use the full banner width to remain readable.

Gale Wings has no full-HP restriction

Parental Bond follow-up hit

Mega Sol is a custom ability

Custom change
Gen 2

The holder uses supported moves as though harsh sunlight were active, including Solar Beam, Weather Ball, recovery and damage adjustments. It does not set field weather for both sides.

Supreme Overlord scales with fallen allies

Modern rule
Gen 2
Abilities do not exist.
Crimson Crystal
Damage increases by 10% for each fainted non-Egg ally, up to five allies and a 1.5× multiplier.

Palafin has no Zero to Hero transformation

Special exception

Palafin uses its listed game stats and abilities without transforming into Hero Form. Original stats refer to its official base form.
Palafin comparison

Flash Fire raises the higher offensive stat

Custom change
Gen 2
Abilities do not exist.
Crimson Crystal
Absorbing a Fire move raises the holder’s higher current Attack or Special Attack by one stage. Ties raise Special Attack. These boosts can stack; this differs from the official Fire-damage-only boost.

Weight abilities are omitted

Special exception
Gen 2
Abilities do not exist.
Crimson Crystal
Heavy Metal and Light Metal are omitted because this battle engine has no Pokémon-weight mechanics. Low Kick uses its fixed-power rules listed above.

Move-by-move updates

Special exception
Gen 2
Hyper Beam still requires recharge after knocking out its target.

Hyper Beam and Giga Impact end without recharge when they knock out the target, following the Gen 1-style rule. A surviving target still causes recharge.

Custom change
Gen 2

Crimson Crystal

Hidden Power always has 70 base power. Its move-table placeholder is not its actual battle power.

Hidden Power chooses its category dynamically

Custom change
Gen 2
Hidden Power’s damage category follows its type.
Crimson Crystal
It becomes physical when the user’s current Attack exceeds Special Attack, and special otherwise. Stat-stage changes are considered; ties use special.

Octazooka lowers both defenses

Custom change
Gen 2
Octazooka has 65 power, 85% accuracy and a chance to lower accuracy.
Crimson Crystal
Octazooka has 90 power, 100% accuracy and a 20% chance to lower Defense and Special Defense together by one stage.

Teleport retains escape behavior

Giga Hammer and Blood Moon have a repeat-use lock

Explosion and Selfdestruct use normal Defense

Protect becomes harder to repeat

Modern rule

Crimson Crystal
Repeated Protect uses approximately 1/3, 1/9, 1/27 and so on. A different action resets the chain.

Spikes stack in three layers

Modern rule
Gen 2

Crimson Crystal
Spikes stacks up to three layers: 1/8, 1/6 and 1/4 maximum HP entry damage. Flying types and other ungrounded Pokémon avoid it.

Weather Ball changes in battle

Sound moves bypass Substitute
